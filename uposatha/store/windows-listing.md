## Description

Six Uposatha days a month, the 8th, 14th and 15th, as the Buddha names them. Real lunar days, the time for food, the parts of the day and night, each rule from the suttas.

THE DAY BEGINS IN THE EVENING
In the suttas a day is "night and day" (AN 3.70), so an Uposatha begins at sunset and lasts through the night and the next day. The app gives the exact moments for your place: when it begins, what it covers, when it ends.

REAL LUNAR DAYS
A lunar day changes at a moment, not at midnight, and lasts 19 to 26 hours. The app reads it from the real Moon (astronomy-engine, accurate to about a minute), and when a lunar day is skipped or repeats against the calendar, it says so under the date.

MEALS: THE TIME FOR FOOD AND VIKĀLA
The card shows how long is left until midday, and after it, vikāla, "the wrong time", until dawn (Pc 37). Midday by the sun or by the clock.

THE THREE PARTS OF THE DAY AND OF THE NIGHT
Morning, midday, evening; first, middle and last watch (MN 53, SN 28.1). The app divides today's daylight and darkness for your place, marks the part going on now, and shows how the monks of the suttas spend it: alms, seclusion, meeting; walking, the lion's posture, rising.

READ THE SOURCE
"From the suttas" and "Key suttas": about 20 passages in Pali and English (AN 3.70 on the cowherd's, the Nigaṇṭha's and the Noble One's Uposatha, AN 10.46, MN 146, Kd 2, MN 65, MN 66, MN 70 and more). "Read" opens the sutta at the exact line in Dhamma.Gift.

WIDGETS FOR THE WINDOWS 11 WIDGETS BOARD
Three widgets, each in three sizes: the next Uposatha with a countdown and the Moon as it is now; the part of the day with kāla and vikāla and the scale from sunrise to sunrise; the month calendar with the Uposatha days marked. Press Win+W, choose Add widgets, then Uposatha by the Suttas.

Thai, Sri Lankan and Burmese communities keep calendars by tradition that can differ by a day. If you keep the Uposatha with a monastery, follow its calendar.
